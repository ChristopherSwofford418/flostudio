# FloStudio Creative Production Expansion

## Research signals

Current category leaders make creative production a structured sequence rather than a prompt box. Arcads positions ad making around selecting a model, starting from performance formats, then editing and remixing the output through specific tools such as product showcase, camera angle, captions, and actor changes.[1] Creatify presents a complementary path: discover a creative direction, create in a selected format, and then launch, test, and optimize across placements.[2] Pencil makes the operational value explicit: creative work progresses through generate, refine, adapt, localize, version, approve, and reuse, with intermediate steps saved for inspection.[3]

## FloStudio’s original extension

FloStudio will keep its portfolio-scale advantage while turning the Portfolio Ad Room into a **creative recipe desk**. An operator selects a product, a proven ad format, a campaign objective, a visual lens, and a compact brief. One bounded real image is produced per take for reliable delivery. The saved output then becomes an actionable campaign artifact that can be sent to the review queue without downloading, reuploading, or recreating it.

The design adds four interoperable layers. **Campaign objective** states the business job: acquire, convert, activate, or announce. **Visual lens** states the cinematic execution: product in use, transformation proof, creator-native, or editorial aspiration. **Product truth** remains explicit through the selected app and optional reference upload. **Review handoff** turns a completed creative into a real pending post and attaches the media asset in the same action.

## Implementation guardrails

The output remains honest: there are no sample ad thumbnails, fake performance values, or simulated review states. AI images are stored in the tenant-scoped media library first. Review posts are created only when the operator explicitly sends an asset to the queue, and the media record is attached to that new post. One image per take is intentional: it keeps real provider calls inside the deployment window and makes creative evolution visible across saved rounds.
